# Audit Report — wow-class-tracker (2026-07-24)

## 0. Scope and framing

This audit was commissioned with an explicit product framing, and everything below is
weighted against it:

> **JTBD-1** — *"Stay up to date on spec changes, both broadly (what moved across the
> field?) and specifically (what exactly changed for my spec?)."*
> **JTBD-2** — *"See how specs stack up against one another from a power and throughput
> perspective for the next season."*

State at audit time: `npm test` 116/116, `npm run build` clean at 680.3 KB / 40 specs /
29 PTR-tracked, newest commit the 2026-07-24 nightly, run manifest 17 `success` /
6 `unreachable` (the upstream WCL rDPS outage plus a Wowhead r.jina.ai 403). The
2026-07-23 audit's dispositions were treated as closed and not re-litigated, except where
a fix was verified **not** to have landed — one such case is recorded below.

## 1. Verdict

**The engineering is in good health; the product is one structural step away from serving
the two jobs it was built for.**

The machinery holds up under adversarial pressure. Forty injection payloads pushed through
every agent-writable field and driven in a real browser did not move the escaping boundary
an inch. Honest source typing is at zero violations across 359 tier values and 623 metric
rows; the creator specialist/generalist firewall is at zero violations across 178 takes and
87 metaNotes; the anomaly-ack firewall is structurally agent-proof; publish never trusts the
agent's build. Fifteen days into an upstream API outage, 167 frozen rows sit at one `asOf`
with zero date regressions and a parallel raw-DPS series that has never once impersonated
the frozen one. That discipline is the project's real asset.

What the audit found is a different failure class than "something is broken today". It is
**instrumentation that measures the wrong thing, and a page organised around what the data
*is* rather than around what *changed*.**

On the engineering side: four staleness gates read a date an agent hand-writes rather than
the date the data carries; the freshness heartbeat structurally cannot detect a single
missed night; the per-fight gate counts 17 encounters instead of the 680 tier rows beneath
them; and no gate constrains a metric *value* at all. Alongside those sit four genuine
published-output defects — a one-character regex gap that publishes a Blizzard buff as a
nerf, bug-fix notes scored as power buffs (promoting a spec a full tier in the tracker's own
forecast), 13 projection tier moves computed nightly and then discarded, and a per-fight
view that silently overrides both the source toggle and the era gate while leaving the
overridden button lit.

On the product side: the two deltas that *are* the owner's jobs are both second-class
citizens. "What changed" is a non-clickable strip 773px down the page (1,658px on a phone)
whose arrows go silent in four of the six views and whose text contradicts the grid beneath
it in the other three. "How the field moves from 12.0.7 into 12.1" — the compressed answer
to JTBD-2, where **44 of 80 (spec, bracket) pairs change tier letter** — is fully computed,
visible per-spec, and has no field-wide surface at all.

Nothing found threatens the integrity of the data pipeline. The risks are of the "quiet
degradation goes unnoticed", "a computed view publishes another view's numbers", and "the
answer is in the payload but the user has to assemble it" varieties.

# Part 1 — UX / product design review

*Seven design lenses (broad-update, per-spec-update, next-season power, visual craft, accessibility, responsive, first-time comprehension), all driven against the real built page in Chromium.*

## Design verdict

This is a genuinely well-made information product, and the thing it does best is the hardest thing: **it never lies about what it knows.** The five-source consensus, the `±` divergence glyph (a shape, not a color — the CSS comment at `src/template.html:170-172` explains exactly why), the amber `.setrow.stale` treatment on frozen rows, the "measured numbers stay numbers" promise on the Ladder, the dashed-gold outline that makes the projection *look* derived — these are real design inventions, not decoration, and Compare's header line ("measured numbers stay numbers, and the 12.1 forecast stays labeled as ours", `template.html:1503`) is the single best sentence on the site. The dark palette is measurably sound (every text token clears AA; `--ink-faint` is 4.89:1 on the lightest surface), reduced-motion is honored end-to-end down to the starfield's rAF loop, and the Finder and Ladder are textbook modal dialogs with real focus traps.

The one structural thing holding it back is this: **the page is organized around what the data IS, not around what changed.** Every surface is a static state — a tier column, a numbers box, a forecast — and the two deltas that actually answer the owner's jobs are second-class citizens. "What changed" is a collapsed strip buried 773px down (1,658px on a phone) whose rows dead-end and whose arrows go silent in four of the six views. "How the field moves from 12.0.7 into 12.1" — the compressed answer to JTBD-2 — is fully computed, per-spec visible, and has no field-wide surface at all. A daily-check product whose change narration is below the fold, non-clickable, and view-inconsistent is paying for its honesty in scroll depth without getting the payoff.

## Job 1 — stay current (broad: "what moved across the field?")

### 🔴 high · Movement narration is consensus-only, and in every other view it contradicts the grid it sits on top of
`movementFor` (`src/render.mjs:421-446`) diffs consensus tiers, metric ranks and the Dummy Dome rank — never `prev.projection`, even though `snapshotStateOf` stores it. Diffing the committed snapshots: **13 projection tier moves 07-23→07-24 vs 9 consensus moves** (Brewmaster raid B→A and M+ B→A, Preservation Evoker M+ B→A, Havoc M+ A→B, Resto Druid raid A+→A). In the "Ours: 12.1 ✦" view `document.querySelectorAll('#matrix .mv').length === 0`, because `showMv = state.source==="consensus" && !state.fight` (`template.html:1026`). Worse, the strip is a boot-time IIFE (`template.html:1541-1554`) that hardcodes the "now" letter as `x.s.consensus?.[b]?.tier` with no "consensus" label and never re-renders — so with Source = Ours: 12.1 the page reads *"Protection Warrior M+ B → C ▼ since Jul 23"* directly above a grid row showing **M+ = B**. Same mismatch in the Icy Veins and Archon views.

**Fix** — (a) add `projMovement` per bracket in `movementFor` comparing `s.projection[b].tier` to `prev.projection[b].tier` with the same `bandIdx` step math; do **not** add it to `baselineDiffers` (`render.mjs:404-416`) — baseline selection must stay tier/rank-grained. (b) Make `showMv` true for `state.source==="projection"` and render `s.projMovement`. (c) Move the strip out of the boot IIFE into `render()` so it is source-aware, and label its lines ("Consensus M+ B → C"). (d) Show the score delta in the hover title — 6 of the 13 moves were ≤2 points across a band edge, so projection arrows are noisier than consensus arrows and must say so. Era-gate the projection lines out of the 12.0.7 view like every other projection surface. Add a render test pinning that a projection change makes an arrow and a consensus-only change does not make a projection arrow. **effort: medium** · `src/render.mjs`, `src/template.html`

*Corrections to the raw finding: "nothing on the page says so" is slightly too strong — the drawer Timeline sparkline draws the projection in gold (`template.html:1422-1431`) and `src/digest.mjs:39-40` reports projection moves in the owner's nightly email. Neither narrates in the matrix.*
